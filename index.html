Abstract

Our world is made up of many different things, from the macro scale, that we see in out everyday lives,
to the micro scale, that cannot be seen by the human eye, our world is a place of many wonders. In this
article, the microscopic world will be discussed, including the two types of the smallest particles known 
in physics, these particles are called fundamental particles and the particles some of them make up.
Alongside the fundamental particles, the forces that allow particles to interact and form that the
particles will take.

Introduction

“Particle physics is the study of the fundamental constituents of matter and their interactions.” 
(Houde, 2021), the theory that is best suited for elementary particles is called the Standard
Model (Houde, 2021). “The exploration of subatomic physics started in 1896 with Becquerel's
discovery of radioactivity; since then, it has been a constant source of surprises, unexpected
phenomena, and fresh insights into the law of Nature.” (Houde, 2021).

Analysis and discussion


It is well known there are many things that make up the subatomic world, for example particles
from “the Standard Model, the fundamental fermionic constitutents of matter are the quarks and
the leptons” (Donoghue, Golowich and Holstein, 2014) and dark matter and energy which currently
have unknown sources (Donoghue, Golowich and Holstein, 2014). The matter we are aware of is made
up of atoms (Bettini and Alessandro, 2024). “Atoms are made of electrons and nuclei bound by the 
-electromagnetic force, whose quantum is the photon” (Bettini and Alessandro, 2024). “Photons are 
massless. Their charge is zero and therefore they do not interact between themselves.” (Bettini 
and Alessandro, 2024).

“Nuclei contain most of the mass of the atoms, and hence of the matter. They are positively charged
and made of protons and neutrons. Protons and neutrons have similar masses, slightly less than a
GeV.” (Bettini and Alessandro, 2024). Collectively protons and neutrons are called nucleons (Bettini
and Alessandro, 2024). These nucleons are known as baryons which are a group of hadrons, another group
of hadrons are called mesons, which are both made up of quarks (Bettini and Alessandro, 2024). Both
baryons and mesons have a quantity called baryon number (B), baryons have a baryon number of 1
(B = 1),and mesons have a baryon number of 0 (B = 0), (Morii, Lim and Mukherjee, 2004). Electrons
are also part of a group; they are classed as leptons (Bettini and Alessandro, 2024). “There are 
three charged leptons, the electron e, the muon µ and the tau τ, and three neutral leptons, the 
neutrinos, one for each of the charged ones. The electron is stable, the µ and the τ decay in 
lighter particles, and neutrinos do not decay.” (Bettini and Alessandro, 2024). “For every 
particle there is an antiparticle with the same mass and the same lifetime and, if it has any 
charge, these have opposite values: the positron for the electron, the antiproton, the antiquarks” 
(Bettini and Alessandro, 2024) “There are six quarks, which fall into two classes” (Donoghue, 
Golowich and Holstein, 2014), this is sorted according to each of their electrical charges (Q).

proton and neutron have almost the same properties” this is because the two are an example of 
particles that are made up of quarks (Houde, 2021). Protons are made up of three quarks being
two up quarks and one down quark, resulting in the proton’s positive charge. Neutrons also are
made up of three quarks like the proton, due to the neutron’s neutral charge, it is made up of
two down quarks and one up quark, (Morii, Lim and Mukherjee, 2004). “As say above, an atom 
consists of an extremely small, positively charged nucleus surrounded by a cloud of negatively
charged electrons” (Houde, 2021). Despite the nucleus being more than 99.9% of the mass of the
atom, it is less than a ten-thousandth the size of the atom (Houde, 2021). Just like there are
6 quarks “there are six leptons which fall into two categories according to their electrical 
charge. The charged leptons e, μ, τ have Q=−e and the neutrinos νe, νμ, ντ have Q=0.” (Donoghue, 
Golowich and Holstein, 2014). 

There are 4 types of interactions between fundamental particles, those 4 interactions are 
electromagnetic, gravitational (which is are incredibly weak), strong, and weak, (Morii, 
Lim and Mukherjee, 2004). The gravitational interaction or gravitational force is the 
attraction between two particles, (Fayyazuddin and Raizuddin, 2011). “It controls the
motion of planets and galaxies and also governs the law of falling bodies. It is a long
distance force. It determines the overall structure of the Universe” (Fayyazuddin and
Raizuddin, 2011). The weak interaction or weak nuclear force is a “weak force is a short
range force eﬀective over a distance of order of 10-16 cm”, (Fayyazuddin and Raizuddin, 
2011). The electromagnetic interaction or electromagnetic force “acts between two 
electrically charged particles” and is also a long range force, (Fayyazuddin and Raizuddin,
2011). Finally, strong interactions or strong nuclear force, is what causes the binding
between neutrons and protons in a nucleus, (Fayyazuddin and Raizuddin, 2011), “the strong
nuclear force is about 100 times the electromagnetic force. It is a short range force” 
(Fayyazuddin and Raizuddin, 2011).
